**Reflection**: a reflection on your experiences in the final project, focusing on what you learned from the experience, divided into a subsection for each team member.

## Christine 

It felt easy to collaborate with my team, largely because we all shared the same goal (as discussing during our team contract): we wanted to build an impactful app that we would feel proud to show to friends and strangers. A key takeaway for me was the importance of early planning to combat procrastination. By meeting early to discuss the upcoming assignments and distributing tasks then, we mostly avoided the stress of finishing the assignment right before the deadline and I felt much better about getting the assignments done on time compared to previous group projects and assignments. 

I learned more on how to use MongoDB with their search operations when implementing the Recipe actions to search for recipes by ingredients and title query. On the frontend side, the user testing phase was particularly valuable. There are some tasks or features that seem obvious to us as developers, but don't always translate well to the user, so getting user feedback and debriefing with the users was essential to improving our app and ensuring our core features were intuitive. 

## Honorine 

Collaborating in a group was a shift from the individual project, and I had to let go of some tunnel vision in design. I often came in having drafted ideas thoroughly, but teammates would suggest different approaches, which pushed me to hold my ideas more loosely and actually listen instead of focusing on my initial vision. Even though our design meetings initially took longer than expected, they often led us to simpler and more workable solutions. 

Concept modularity made it easier for us to work asynchronously without blocking each other, and one concrete challenge was coordinating syncs across concepts. I spent a lot of time debugging frame binding issues when integrating Recipe actions with Collecting. I learned to test syncs incrementally, verifying each piece worked before connecting everything, which saved time compared to building the whole flow and debugging later. 

I built several skills throughout the project, including collaborative decision-making, incremental testing, and debugging across a shared codebase. I used Claude Code frequently, which was extremely helpful for translating specs into code and debugging. Going forward, I want to improve my scope estimation for team projects and develop a better sense of when something is “good enough.”

## Ryan

Working on the final project has definitely been a very informative and educational experience. Working in a group meant that I had to be a lot more intentional with proposing design choices ahead of time and work on only one section at a time, since I couldn't just "figure it out" and make unilateral design changes. Because of this, I relied a lot less on vibe coding, and actually tried to go about implementing concepts, syncs, and front end elements mainly by hand. I did still use copilot occasionally, but the use was for either general questions (non-agentic), or for implementing specific components of a feature, rather than the whole feature itself. As a result, I definitely learned more about how actions and syncs are implemented, as well as how to interact to interact with MongoDB. 

I also see more of the appeal of concept design now that I've worked on it in a group. It was pretty easy to create and modify concept actions without worrying too much about other aspects of the app, since the concepts were all modular. Side effects were still a concern for syncs, which did constitute a major portion of our project, but it was ultimately still beneficial. 

Additionally, I gained/improved on many skills related to collaboration. Time management and planning ahead of time were definitely major components of this project, since we had multiple major deadlines to meet each week with no leniency, and I'd say we handled it pretty well by setting internal deadlines and meeting/discussing progress multiple times per week. 

## Eyob

Over the course of this second assignment, I saw how powerful these concepts were for both reasoning about problems and problem encapsulation, and, more surprisingly, for collaboration. One of the major features I worked on was the app's backend authentication system. At first, I created just one User concept, but after getting feedback from the team, I split it into three parts: User for account existence, UserAuthentication for credentials and sessions, and Profile for the display name. This change showed me how useful it is to give each part a single responsibility. It made testing easier, and registration became simpler because each part could be created one after another without mixing up different logic. Writing tests also helped me catch bugs early, since I had to handle things like duplicate emails, expired sessions, and invalid formats. Defining the concepts beforehand also allowed the team to work on separate timelines throughout the week, with only minor adjustments needed to make everything work when it was time to put things together.

On the frontend, I worked on layout and navigation. I built the header with breadcrumb navigation and a dropdown menu, refactored the sidebar to remove repeated code, and added a CSS theme file to maintain consistent styling. These little refactors, which are usually the bain of my existence when working on my own projects, I realize now helped us move faster when working in a group since we no longer had to reason about repeated logic in different sections.

I would definitely say that 6.1040 changed the way I coded. It was the first time I experimented with Agentic IDEs and with coding a large, collaborative, full-stack project with more than one other person. While Windsurf could make changing code easy, I couldn’t just make changes and refactor on my own; I needed to share my design decisions with the team first. Having clear, separate concepts helped because I could focus on authentication without worrying about Recipe or Collecting, and we brought everything together during syncs. I realized that good abstractions help with more than just clean code; they make it easier for everyone to work together and use tools to help us code faster.
